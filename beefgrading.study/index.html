        How these rankings work

        Each result is rated from the team's placing, scaled by field size, how recent the contest is, the contest's weight, and field strength (the average rating of the top ten teams there). The solver repeats until field strengths and team ratings agree. A school's rating is the average of its five best results this season. Only real results count, and nothing carries over from other seasons. Schools with too few contests this season stay unranked and show their actual results. Movement compares the board with the one before the most recent contest. New marks a school's first appearance. Individuals mode is different: each contestant, alternates included, is ranked by the average of their overall scores across the season's contests, and needs at least two contests for a numbered rank. The International is scored on a different format and is left out of contestant averages for now.

        Nine callable grades, best to worst. The heavy rules mark family boundaries. One subdivision (High, Average, Low) is one-third of a grade.

        Marbling degrees to grades

        Within A maturity, the marbling score in the ribeye sets the quality grade. Each photo is the official USDA reference for the minimum of its degree. Tap any photo to view it full screen.

        1. Abundant High Prime
        2. Moderately Abundant Average Prime
        3. Slightly Abundant Low Prime
        4. Moderate High Choice
        5. Modest Average Choice
        6. Small Low Choice
        7. Slight¹ Select
        8. Traces & Practically Devoid² Standard

        ¹ Select splits on the upper/lower half of Slight: Slight 50 to 100 grades High Select, Slight 0 to 50 grades Low Select.
        ² USDA publishes no reference photo below Slight; genuinely negligible marbling is rare in fed cattle.
        Photographs: official USDA marbling references (public domain).

        A word on maturity

        Quality grade is marbling plus maturity, so this drill assumes every carcass is A maturity (roughly 9 to 30 months, nearly all fed cattle) and grades it straight off the marbling. In the ring, maturity is read from the actual bones and lean, and building that into this drill is not realistic with the resources available right now. A maturity study tool may be a future addition.
